# Annual weather conditions determined the effects of microbial inoculants on maize performance and the functional profile of rhizosphere microbiota at field scale

doi:

Inoculating crops with beneficial microbes offers a sustainable approach for enhancing plant growth and health. Nevertheless, their broad adoption in agricultural systems remains constrained, primarily due to inconsistent outcomes and insufficient understanding of the underlying mechanisms. Therefore, we investigated the effects of a beneficial microorganism consortium (BMc: Bacillus, Pseudomonas, and Trichoderma strain) on maize plants (growth and gene expression) and the resident microbiomes in three consecutive growing seasons (2020, 2021, and 2022) under reduced tillage and fertilization practices. Inoculants established in the rhizosphere in all three growing seasons, with the lowest abundance in 2022. The results indicated a beneficial effect of BMc inoculation on plant performance under low precipitation rates in 2020. Under well-watered conditions, such as in 2021, the effect was less pronounced, and under extremely dry conditions, such as in 2022, no effect was detectable. Additionally, different microbiome patterns were observed in each growing season, indicating a microbial pattern for high stress in 2022 and a recruitment of potential plant beneficial resident microorganisms in the rhizosphere in 2020. Analyses revealed two metagenomic assembled genomes (MAGs) of Bacillus atrophaeus and Lysobacter antibioticus, which positively responded to inoculation in 2020 and harbored a large repertoire of plant beneficial genes. We conclude that stress tolerance of plants is particularly enhanced by BMc inoculation under abiotic stress conditions such as drought. However, when precipitation falls below a critical threshold or plants experience prolonged periods of drought, BMc inoculation and their mode of action to recruit beneficial resident microorganisms cannot overcome the adverse impact of water limitation on plant performance, which indicates a moisture optimum for beneficial microbes.
